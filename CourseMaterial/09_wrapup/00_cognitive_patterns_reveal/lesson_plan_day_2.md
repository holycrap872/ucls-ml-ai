## Essential Questions

- How do the patterns we've been spotting all year apply to ourselves?
- What does it mean to reason against your own preferences?

## Lesson Plan

### Setup

- Past-self writing (Unit 7 research paper) returned to each student
- `cognitive_patterns_reference.md` projected

### Actual Lesson

- Review
    - Candle problem
    - Grade prediction patterns
    - "Invisible architecture"
- Past-self analysis (~25m)
    - Hand back Unit 7 research paper
    - Prompt: find one place in your own earlier writing where you can now
      identify motivated reasoning or a named pattern from the reference page
    - This is hard — give time and space
    - Not about self-criticism; about noticing
- Small group share (~10m)
    - Groups of 3: share what you found (or didn't find)
    - Was it easier or harder than spotting it in someone else's writing?
- Whole class debrief (~15m)
    - What did you notice?
    - How is this different from spotting patterns in a ChatBot's output?
    - What's the prompting move you'd use on yourself?
    - Final question: "What will you do differently next time you're writing
      something you care about?"
